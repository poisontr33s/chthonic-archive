Material Principle

Tarred hull, not polished brass. The Obsidian Register reads warm — amber and brown, a library fire, the archive as scholar's study. Nassau inverts the warmth without softening the darkness: the background drops its red bias and pulls toward deep ocean blue-black, the color of water under no moon. The depth is (5, 8, 12) — blue-dominant — where the Obsidian system was (12, 8, 6). Same darkness, different undertone. Ocean floor, not cave earth.

The lantern token keeps amber as its root — no ship runs cold light, and readability at that accent weight is correct. But it is darker than the Obsidian fire, less gold: #B87828 against the Obsidian #E8A020. Earned rather than announced. The verdigris displaces bronze: ship copper after a season at sea, the compass housing, the cannon trunnion, anything iron or copper that met salt air long enough to turn.

The wine token — #7A2838 — carries the third axis. Cold aristocratic menace. The presence that is dangerous because it is composed. It does not appear in the Obsidian palette at all; it is native to Nassau. Use at call-to-action weight only, or as a warning state. It is not decorative.

Verdigris — Time as Material Property

Salt turns copper green. The Obsidian system used the word patina and the color #4A3A28 — brown, warm, the archive's age. Nassau replaces brown patina with verdigris: the same temporal function, different material. A leaf untouched for six months is not merely old — it has been at sea. The tint applied to its surface is #3E7A6A, copper-green, the color of oxidization not degradation.

The depth mapping is preserved: recent leaf at stratum 3, ancient leaf toward stratum 1. The emotional valence shifts. Old does not read as dusty here. It reads as weathered. Something that went to sea and came back.
