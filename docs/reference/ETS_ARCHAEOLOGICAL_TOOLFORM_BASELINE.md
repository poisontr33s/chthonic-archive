# ET-S Archaeological Toolform Baseline

This note is a dry-lane research baseline for future SVG work.
It is not a replacement for the ANKH design canon.
It is the archaeology-first pool that later SVG passes can draw from when the
question is not "what looks cool?" but "what did these tool and craft forms
actually look like, and what can be abstracted honestly?"

## Immediate Repo Relevance

Primary near-term consumer:
- [tools.svg (product)](../../extensions/chthonic-archive/themes/icons/product/tools.svg) (codicon ID `tools`, formerly `hammer`)

Secondary consumers:
- future product icon revisions in
  [themes/icons/product](../../extensions/chthonic-archive/themes/icons/product)
- future file/folder SVG passes where forge, masonry, craft, ritual tool, or
  strike-tool language needs firmer archaeological grounding

## Scope

This reference focuses on:
- Egyptian strike, shaping, and woodworking/stoneworking tool forms
- Andean strike, hammered-metal, pin/knife, and stone-fit form language
- early or archaeologically grounded visual traits that can survive 16px icon abstraction

This reference does **not** claim that Egypt and the Andes shared the same tool
types or metallurgical history. The 50/50 ANKH lane is a design synthesis, not
a false historical merger.

## Core Design Conclusion

The repo should **not** default to a modern industrial claw hammer or a generic
rectangular blacksmith hammer when it wants a "forge/build" glyph.

The archaeology pool suggests a better rule:
- Egyptian side gives you the **mallet, pounder, adze, chisel-struck tool, and ritualized handle-blade junction**
- Andean side gives you **hammered sheet curves, semicircular tupu heads, tumi-like crescent geometry, trapezoid/polygonal masonry logic, and bilateral ornamental symmetry**

So the truthful ANKH hammer is usually **not** "Victorian hammer silhouette plus gold paint."
It is more convincing as:
- a mallet/adze hybrid
- a pounder-with-sacred aperture
- a hammer handle joined to a tupu or tumi-derived head geometry
- a strike glyph framed by trapezoid or polygonal stone-fit logic

## Egyptian Pool

### 1. Pounder, Not Claw Hammer

The most archaeologically grounded Egyptian "heavy strike" silhouette is often
not a forged hammer at all, but a blunt pounder or striking stone.

Why this matters:
- it avoids importing a much later industrial silhouette
- it fits quarrying, shaping, and impact labor more honestly
- it survives reduction well at icon scale because it is mass-first, not detail-first

Preserve these traits:
- ovoid or rounded striking body
- short, stable vertical axis
- no split claw
- no narrow steel neck

### 2. Mallet Is the Cleanest Compact Glyph

The British Museum's Egyptian woodworking mallet is much closer to a plausible
icon ancestor than a modern carpenter's hammer.

Preserve these traits:
- thick, simple head
- centered shaft
- low-detail silhouette
- authority through mass, not through mechanical finesse

### 3. Adze Is the Most Distinctively Egyptian Toolform

If the repo wants a work/build/forge glyph that reads as ancient rather than
generic, the adze is one of the strongest candidates.

Preserve these traits:
- transverse blade
- clear right-angle relationship between handle and blade
- visible junction or lashing logic
- asymmetry that still reads at 16px

Use case:
- better than a generic hammer where the intent is "craft authority" or
  "shaping matter into order"

## Andean Pool

### 4. Hammered Sheet Is the Ground Truth

The earliest Andean metallurgy in the Met essay is not cast-tool logic.
It is hammered native metal sheet, then more advanced hammering and gilding.

This means the Andean half should bias toward:
- cut-sheet contours
- crescents
- perforations
- bilateral symmetry
- broad, thin forms instead of thick forged blocks

### 5. Tupu Is More Useful Than a Literal Hammer

The tupu is not a hammer, but it is an extremely useful Andean form for ANKH
icon work because it combines:
- a strong circular or semicircular head
- a center void/perforation
- a long linear shaft

That makes it ideal as a structural donor for:
- sacred handle-head joins
- aperture logic
- badge or product icon simplification

### 6. Tumi and Ornament Curves Belong in the Head, Not the Handle

For a forge/build icon, the Andean contribution should usually shape the
**head** vocabulary:
- crescents
- stepped or cut-sheet edges
- serpent or bird-profile curvature
- central voids and symmetric flares

Avoid turning the entire icon into a literal tumi unless the function is closer
to ritual blade than hammer/build.

### 7. Masonry Geometry Is a Framing System

Inka masonry and trapezoid openings are especially useful when the icon needs
to read as "architectonic" rather than "ornamental."

Preserve these traits:
- taper inward as the form rises
- polygonal fit logic
- nested stone-like framing
- negative space that feels carved, not machined

## Recommended 50/50 Silhouette Directions

### Direction A: Mallet + Trapezoid Aperture

Formula:
- Egyptian base = wooden mallet mass
- Andean modifier = small trapezoid negative space or taper logic inside the head

Why it works:
- still reads as "hammer/build"
- gains Andean architecture without becoming decorative clutter

### Direction B: Adze + Tupu Void

Formula:
- Egyptian base = adze handle plus transverse work edge
- Andean modifier = perforated semicircle or tupu-like aperture at the head junction

Why it works:
- strongest uniqueness
- avoids generic modern hammer entirely

### Direction C: Pounder + Polygonal Frame

Formula:
- Egyptian base = blunt pounder body
- Andean modifier = polygonal masonry frame around or within the strike mass

Why it works:
- excellent at 16px
- reads as force plus stone intelligence

### Direction D: Hammer Head from Hammered Sheet Logic

Formula:
- Egyptian base = vertical shaft and strike authority
- Andean modifier = crescent-cut or stepped sheet-derived head contour

Why it works:
- keeps the semantic category "hammer"
- shifts the silhouette out of modern software-default territory

## Negative Rules

Do not default to:
- claw hammer tails
- modern steel-neck carpenter hammer profiles
- symmetric cartoon blacksmith blocks with no archaeological donor
- tiny ornamental details that only work at full SVG preview size
- fake "ancient" distressing as a substitute for real form lineage

## Practical 16px Extraction Rules

At product-icon scale, preserve in this order:
1. head silhouette
2. handle-head junction
3. one culturally meaningful void, taper, or transverse relation
4. only then minor motif detail

If a feature disappears below 16px, drop it.
The archaeology should survive through **form**, not through decorative micro-lines.

## Design Inference Boundary

Everything above the source stack is a design inference from archaeological
evidence, not a claim that one historical culture literally used the other's
objects.

The honest ANKH move is:
- keep the evidence separate
- abstract the forms carefully
- fuse them only after the archaeological donors are individually legible

That is the ET-S baseline for future hammer and tool SVG work.
